- **What does this plug-in do?** It makes a JPEG XL copy of every image in your media library. Browsers that show JPEG XL get the copy. Incompatible browsers are served your original file.
- **What is JPEG XL and which browsers show it?** JPEG XL is the new standard image format made to follow JPEG (ISO/IEC 18181): smaller files with the same or higher quality. It can hold JPEGs without changing them. Chrome, Firefox and Safari all now support JXL by default.
- **How much smaller will my images be?** It depends on your photos, the quality mode selected and the effort level, which sets how much time is spent working to decrease file size. Testing on various photos using "Same look" at the quality 90 default made them about 75% smaller than the uploaded JPEG. "Exact" made files about 18% smaller with the exact same pixels.
- **Will my images look worse?** No. In "Same look", each copy is made to look like the original at your quality number and checked for visible differences with SSIMULACRA 2. At the default effort, the look is measured on every image and the encoder keeps the smallest file that can match it. "Exact" keeps every pixel unchanged. "Size limit" keeps the best look under the size you set.
- **What happens to my original files?** Nothing is ever lost. By default, JPEGs are kept as JPEG XL, then converted back losslessly into the identical JPEG when needed. Other formats always keep the original file. Switch on "Keep original JPEG files" to keep JPEGs too, which is standard on Cloudflare's Free plan due to its limitations.
- **Do I need an account, key or a subscription? Are my images sent anywhere?** No. Images are converted on your own server, or in your own browser where the server cannot handle it. rapier-jxl is free for anybody to use in their own site under the MIT open source license. Nothing is uploaded and there are no usage limits.
- **What about images already on my site or imported from WordPress?** The plug-in's page shows how many older images are waiting, and you can choose to convert them in the background. "Import from WordPress" on the same page brings in a WordPress export's images, updates the links in your posts and converts them like any upload.
- **Which images does it convert and where?** JPEG, PNG, WebP and AVIF; GIF and SVG are left as they are. On a Node server, everything but WebP and AVIF converts on the server in the background. On Cloudflare, the Worker stores JPEGs losslessly and most other conversions run in your browser while the plug-in's page is open (all of them on the Free plan).
- **How long does converting take? What is Effort?** Converting runs in the background after each upload, so uploading is not slower. Effort, from 1 to 9, is how hard the encoder works for a smaller file. The default, 7, makes the smallest files at your quality and takes about a minute and a half for a 12-megapixel photo on a small 4-core server. If converting feels slow, lower the Effort level for faster conversion with larger files. At effort 3, the same photo takes about a second and its look is estimated instead of measured to save time.
- **Does it work with responsive images?** Yes. Sizes makes smaller copies for small screens (widths up to 3840 pixels by default), all JPEG XL.
- **Does it work with my CDN or page cache?** Image responses carry `Vary: Accept`, so any cache that honors it keeps the JPEG XL copy and the original separate.
- **How does it work?** rapier-jxl, a JPEG XL encoder written in JavaScript and WebAssembly, runs inside your site. A JPEG's compressed data is first repacked losslessly. For "Same look", the encoder makes the file, measures it against the original and keeps the smallest one that still looks right. Each copy is decoded and checked against its original before it is served.
- **How do I remove it?** Run `npx rapier-jxl remove emdash` in your site's folder. It stops new copies, rebuilds and checks every original JPEG, then removes the plug-in from `astro.config.mjs`.
- **What storage works?** Cloudflare R2 and Node local storage.
